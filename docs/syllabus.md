## 1. Exam format (Grades 1–5, online)

Source: [SPEC] pp. 9–10 and 25–26; [REF].

- **Delivery:** online only, on a laptop or desktop with a webcam. The candidate is recorded and proctored afterwards (not live). Paper exams are only for access needs or reasonable adjustments.
- **Duration:** Grades 1–3 last **1½ hours** maximum and Grades 4–5 last **2 hours** maximum. A countdown timer shows on every screen.
- **Allowed:** blank rough paper (plain or manuscript), shown to the camera at the start and destroyed at the end. No notes or books. Any piano keyboard or picture of one in the room must be covered.
- **Marking:** automatic. Results are released after the video has been reviewed.
- **Structure:** **7 sections**, each made up of several questions. The total is **75 marks**. The marks for each question appear in brackets on the right of the screen.

### Sections and marks

The section names come from the [SPEC] p. 26 marking criteria ("Learners are assessed on") and the order of the 2020 sample papers.

| # | Grades 1–3 section | Marks (G1–3) | Grades 4–5 section | Marks (G4–5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rhythm | 15 | Rhythm | **10** |
| 2 | Pitch (and transposition) | 15 | Pitch and transposition | **10** |
| 3 | Keys and scales | 15 | Keys and scales | **15** |
| 4 | Intervals | 10 | Intervals | **10** |
| 5 | Tonic triads | 10 | Triads and chords | **10** |
| 6 | Terms and signs | 5 | Terms, signs and instruments | **10** |
| 7 | Music in context | 5 | Music in context | **10** |
| | **Total** | **75** | **Total** | **75** |

[SPEC] p. 25 gives the numbers for each section. The pairing of names with section numbers is my inference, based on the order of the list on p. 26 and the 2020 sample papers, where section 1 is "Rhythm", 2 "Pitch", 3 "Keys and Scales", 6 "Terms and Signs" and 7 "Music in Context". It is consistent.

### Question formats (from [REF], Grade 5)

1. **Multiple choice.** Click one option, then click **Answer** (for example, "Select the correct time signature for the bar", with 3 options).
2. **Drag and drop.** Drag a note-head onto the stave. There are **five note-heads: natural, sharp, flat, double sharp, double flat**. The note can be repositioned, and an accurate placement is required (for example, "form an interval of a compound perfect 5th above the given note"; no ledger lines needed in that example).
3. **Fill in the blank.** Type **digits only** (1, 2, 3, not "one"). Tab moves to the next field (for example, "In [time sig] there are __ dotted-quaver beats in a bar"; "A breve is equal to __ crotchet(s)").
4. **Matching / pull-down.** Choose from a drop-down list for every part (A–E) before **Answer** becomes active (for example, choosing suitable chords for two cadences from **I, II, IV or V** in five boxes).

Other conventions:
- Each "Answer" click confirms the response and moves to the next question ([REF] wording: "click Answer to confirm your response and move to the next question"). **[REF] does not say whether candidates can go back to earlier questions. Not confirmed.**
- The info page says: "There are seven sections in this paper. The total mark is 75."
- The 2020 sample papers (G1–3) also use tick-one-box, circle true/false, tick/cross each box, and "complete this sentence by adding a number". These are the printed equivalents of the online formats.

**Terminology table shown on the info screen [REF]:** bar = measure; breve = double whole note; semibreve = whole note; minim = half note; crotchet = quarter note; quaver = 8th note; semiquaver = 16th note; demisemiquaver = 32nd note.

## 2. Grade 5 content

The syllabus is **cumulative**: Grade 5 is "As in preceding grades, with the addition of: …" ([OUT], [SPEC] p. 13). The [SPEC] p. 16 tables are "presented cumulatively, i.e. once introduced, they apply to all subsequent grades."

### 2a. Grade 5 syllabus text, verbatim ([OUT] = [SPEC] p. 13)

> **Grade 5** As in preceding grades, with the addition of:
> 1. Irregular time signatures of 5/4, 7/4, 5/8 and 7/8, and the grouping of notes and rests within these times. Irregular divisions of simple time values.
> 2. Tenor clef (C clef centred on 4th line). The identification of notes in the four clefs in any of the keys set for this grade (see below), and the transposition at the octave of a simple melody from any clef to another. Transposition to concert pitch of a short melody notated for an instrument in B♭, A or F, and vice versa (the interval of transposition up or down will be given).
> 3. Scales and key signatures of all major and minor keys up to and including six sharps and flats. All simple and compound intervals from any note.
> 4. The root position (a), 1st inversion (b) and 2nd inversion (c) forms of the tonic, supertonic, subdominant and dominant chords in any of the keys set for this grade. The choice of suitable chords at cadential points of a simple melody in the major key of C, G, D or F. Perfect, plagal and imperfect cadences in the major keys of C, G, D or F.
> 5. More terms and signs. The recognition of ornaments, including the replacement of written-out ornamentation with the appropriate signs, but not vice versa. Questions on the types of voice and names of instruments, the clefs they use, instrument family groups and the basic way by which they produce sound, as well as points of general musical observation designed to test the candidate's ability to apply theoretical knowledge to actual music.

### 2b. Grades 1–4 syllabus text, verbatim (all still examinable at Grade 5)

> **Grade 1**
> 1. Note values of semibreve, minim, crotchet, quaver and semiquaver, and their equivalent rests (candidates may use the terms 'whole note', 'half note', etc.). Tied notes. Single-dotted notes.
> 2. Simple time signatures of 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, bar-lines and the grouping of the notes listed above within these times.
> 3. The stave. Treble (G) and bass (F) clefs. Names of notes on the stave, including middle C in both clefs. Sharp, flat and natural signs, and their cancellation.
> 4. Construction of the major scale, including the position of the tones and semitones. Scales and key signatures of the major keys of C, G, D and F in both clefs, with their tonic triads (root position), degrees (number only), and intervals above the tonic (by number only).
> 5. Some frequently used terms and signs concerning tempo, dynamics, performance directions and articulation marks. Simple questions will be asked about a melody written in either treble or bass clef.
>
> **Grade 2** As in Grade 1, with the addition of:
> 1. Simple time signatures of 2/2, 3/2, 4/2 and 3/8 and the grouping of notes and rests within these times. Triplets, and triplet note groups with rests.
> 2. Extension of the stave to include two ledger lines below and above each stave.
> 3. Relative major and minor keys. Construction of the minor scale (harmonic only). Scales and key signatures of the major keys of A, B♭ and E♭, and the minor keys of A, E and D, with their tonic triads (root position), degrees (number only), and intervals above the tonic (by number only).
> 4. More terms and signs in common use.
>
> **Grade 3** As in preceding grades, with the addition of:
> 1. Compound time signatures of 6/8, 9/8, 12/8 and the grouping of notes and rests within these times. The demisemiquaver (32nd note) and its equivalent rest.
> 2. Extension of the stave beyond two ledger lines. Transposition at the octave from the treble clef to the bass clef, and vice versa.
> 3. Scales and key signatures of all major and minor keys up to and including four sharps and flats, including both harmonic and melodic forms of minor scales, with their tonic triads (root position), degrees (number only), and intervals above the tonic (number and type).
> 4. More terms and signs.
>
> **Grade 4** As in preceding grades, with the addition of:
> 1. All simple and compound duple, triple and quadruple time signatures, and the grouping of notes and rests within these times. The breve and its equivalent rest. Double-dotted notes and rests. Duplets.
> 2. Alto clef (C clef centred on 3rd line). Notes in the alto clef in any of the keys set for this grade (see below). Notes of the same pitch written in different clefs (treble, alto, bass) and transposition at the octave from the treble or the bass clef to the alto clef, and vice versa. Double sharp and double flat signs, and their cancellation. Enharmonic equivalents.
> 3. Scales and key signatures of all major and minor keys up to and including five sharps and flats, with both forms of minor scales. Technical names for the notes of the diatonic scale (tonic, supertonic, etc.). Construction of the chromatic scale. All intervals, not exceeding an octave, between any two diatonic notes in any of the keys set for this grade.
> 4. Triads (root position) on the tonic, subdominant and dominant notes in any of the keys set for this grade. Chords in root position on the tonic, subdominant and dominant notes in any of the keys set for this grade (the harmonic form of the scale will be used in minor keys).
> 5. More terms and signs, including the recognition and naming of the trill, turn, upper and lower mordent, acciaccatura and appoggiatura. Questions about a passage of music will include simple related questions about standard orchestral instruments.

Instrument background facts implied by "clefs they use / family / how sound is produced / single or double reed / transposing". These are general knowledge, **not listed item by item by ABRSM**:
- **Clefs:** violin treble; viola alto (treble for high passages); cello bass (tenor and treble for high); double bass bass (sounds an octave lower than written); flute, oboe, clarinet, trumpet, horn, piccolo, cor anglais treble (horn sometimes bass); bassoon bass (tenor for high); trombone bass and tenor (alto in some music); tuba and timpani bass; harp and piano treble and bass (grand stave); xylophone, glockenspiel, celesta, tubular bells, vibraphone and marimba treble (marimba sometimes bass too).
- **Reeds:** clarinet = single reed; oboe, cor anglais and bassoon = double reed; flute and piccolo = no reed (edge-blown).
- **Transposing (written-to-sounding):** clarinet in B♭ sounds a major 2nd lower; clarinet in A sounds a minor 3rd lower; horn in F sounds a perfect 5th lower; trumpet in B♭ sounds a major 2nd lower; cor anglais (in F) sounds a perfect 5th lower. Octave transposers are piccolo (sounds an octave higher), double bass (sounds an octave lower), glockenspiel (2 octaves higher), xylophone (an octave higher) and celesta (an octave higher). The [SPEC] only says "transposing/non-transposing", and in the exam "the interval of transposition … will be given". Treat these intervals as background, not as items candidates must recall.
- **Definite pitch:** timpani, xylophone, marimba, glockenspiel, vibraphone, celesta, tubular bells. **Indefinite pitch:** side drum, bass drum, cymbals, triangle, tambourine, castanets, tam-tam.

## 4. Ambiguities and uncertainties

1. **English meanings are not in any free official ABRSM document.** [SPEC] and [TS] list the terms only. The meanings above follow ABRSM house style, and 15 are confirmed verbatim from the 2020 model answers (adagio, decrescendo, mp, staccato, ♩ = 120; molto, dolce, dal segno, semi-staccato, allegretto ("fairly quick"); alla marcia, prestissimo, sempre, tenuto ("slight pressure"), allargando ("broadening")). For the rest, accept close synonyms: in the online exam the meaning is chosen from multiple-choice options, so exact wording is not required. If exact ABRSM wording matters, check against *Discovering Music Theory* Grades 1–5 (ABRSM, 2020), which I couldn't access.
2. **Grade 2 sign count:** [SPEC] p. 18 lists **8** G2 signs, but [TS] says "24 Terms & **9** Signs". I couldn't see the 9th sign in the text transcription; [TS]'s signs are images. A likely explanation: ¢ appears separately under time signatures at G2, or "8va"/"8vb" are counted separately. Treat the [SPEC] 8 as authoritative and the 9th as unconfirmed.
3. **Septuplets** are not named. The text says "irregular divisions of simple time values" and the table names only **quintuplet; sextuplet** (plus duplets at G4 and triplets at G2). Safest: quintuplets and sextuplets for G5. Septuplets are not confirmed and should not be tested.
4. **"All simple and compound duple, triple and quadruple time signatures" (G4)**, but the content table lists only 4/8, 6/4, 9/4, 6/16, 9/16, 12/16 as new. So 2/8, 3/16, 2/16, 4/16, 12/4 and similar are covered by the text but not shown in the table. Prefer the listed ones, and treat the others as possible but low-likelihood.
5. **Pitch ranges** for each clef and grade are given only as range diagrams. My descriptions (about two ledger lines at G2, three at G3, four for octave transposition at G4) are approximate. G3 transposition says "up to three ledger lines" and G4 "up to four ledger lines". G5 gives no explicit ledger-line limit.
6. **Interval list at G5:** the chromatic intervals named (aug/dim 3rd, aug/dim 6th, aug 7th, aug/dim 8ve) plus G4's diatonic set do not explicitly include **augmented unison, diminished 2nd, doubly augmented/diminished** intervals. The text says "All simple and compound intervals from any note", which is broad. Safest: major, minor, perfect, augmented and diminished intervals from 2nd to 15th (no unisons and no doubly altered intervals). The [SPEC] G6 table adds "chromatic semitone (augmented unison)" at G6, which suggests it is **not** a G5 item.
7. **Section names and numbers:** [SPEC] gives marks per section number and a separate list of assessed areas. I inferred the pairing in the section 1 table from the list order and the 2020 sample papers. It is consistent but not stated explicitly for G4–5.
8. **Cadence keys vs chord keys:** chords I, II, IV, V and their inversions are in **any key up to 6♯/6♭**, but cadences and the cadential chord choice are restricted to **C, G, D, F major**. For cadences ABRSM doesn't say which inversions may appear (for example Ic–V). The [REF] example offers only the chord names I, II, IV, V.
9. **Chromatic scale spelling:** [SPEC] says "construction of the chromatic scale" without specifying the spelling convention ("harmonic" vs "melodic" chromatic). Accept any spelling that does not use the same letter name more than twice in succession and ascends/descends by semitones. ABRSM model answers generally accept any correct chromatic spelling, but I couldn't verify this for 2020.
10. **Minor keys and chords:** G4 explicitly says "the harmonic form of the scale will be used in minor keys" for chords (so V is major, with a raised leading note). G5 doesn't repeat this, but it carries over cumulatively.
11. **[TS] provenance:** the original ABRSM-hosted file (gb.abrsm.org) couldn't be fetched from this environment. I only read a third-party text transcription, which matches the [SPEC] lists exactly apart from the G2 sign count.
12. **Andantino:** historically ambiguous (some sources say "a little slower than andante"). ABRSM materials use "slightly faster than andante".
13. **Spelling variants in the official list:** "leggiero" (not "leggero"), "sostenuto, sost" (no full stop), "mässig" (with ä), "tristamente" (as printed). Keep these exactly as printed.
